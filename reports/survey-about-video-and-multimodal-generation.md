# Survey of Video and Multimodal Generation

## TL;DR
- Video generation has evolved from short clips to minute-long, high-fidelity videos with world-simulation capabilities, driven by diffusion models and transformer architectures. [1][2][3]
- Multimodal generation integrates text, image, audio, and video through unified foundation models, enabling cross-modal tasks like text-to-video and audio-visual separation. [4][5][6]
- Key challenges remain in temporal coherence, motion-appearance coupling, and efficient long-video synthesis, addressed by autoregressive models, world models, and temporal self-supervision. [7][8][9][10]
- Evaluation has shifted to multi-dimensional benchmarks (VBench, Video-Bench, PEBench) that decompose quality into fine-grained aspects and align with human judgment. [11][12][13][14][15][16][17]
- Recent advances include scalable diffusion transformers (Sora, Veo 2), efficient open-source alternatives (Open-Sora), and streaming world models for real-time applications. [18][3][19][20][21]

## Background
Video and multimodal generation aim to synthesize coherent sequences across modalities (text, image, video, audio) from prompts or other conditions. Foundational surveys present a hierarchical, generative-first taxonomy with four layers: Foundations (multimodal fusion strategies, embedding representations), Mechanisms (GANs, Diffusion Models, Contrastive Pretraining, MLLMs), Applications (text-to-video, etc.), and Higher-level Capabilities (multimodal understanding, synthesis, adaptive content). [6][5][22][23] The field matters now due to the explosion of video content and the rise of multimodal foundation models that enable unified generation pipelines. [1][4] Key challenges identified in foundational work include alignment, temporal coherence, motion-appearance coupling, and limited supervision for temporal aspects. [1]

## Foundations and Taxonomies
Foundational taxonomies link multimodal fusion strategies (early, late, hybrid) and embedding representations to core mechanisms such as GANs, diffusion models, contrastive pretraining, and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). [6][5][22][23] Six categories of generative AI architectures for multimodal content are identified: cross-modal and modality-specific generation, translation and alignment, multimodal data fusion and representation learning, multimodal content understanding and synthesis, personalization and human-centered generation, and human–AI collaboration and co-creation. [6] Multimodal data fusion and representation learning enable joint processing and alignment of information from diverse modalities through unified embedding spaces. [6] In video generation specifically, pretrained video models often fail to reliably follow human intent, maintain temporal coherence, or satisfy physical and safety constraints, with alignment facing unique challenges including error accumulation over time, motion-appearance coupling, multi-objective trade-offs, and limited supervision for temporal aspects. [1]

## Main Approaches
Diffusion models dominate high-fidelity video generation due to strong controllability, competitive visual quality, and compatibility with multimodal inputs, but face temporal coherence and efficiency challenges. [24][8][25] A unified taxonomy categorizes over two hundred diffusion video generation methods by characteristics, strengths, and limitations. [25] Autoregressive models, exemplified by NOVA, achieve high efficiency without vector quantization, surpassing prior models and diffusion methods in various tasks with fewer parameters, offering an efficient alternative for long-video synthesis. [7] GAN-based video generation struggles with temporal coherence, which can be improved via temporal self-supervision using novel losses and metrics. [9] Transformer-based and world modeling approaches play significant roles, particularly in modeling causal dynamics of scenes (e.g., surgical video generation) and enabling procedural task execution through closed-loop systems that adapt to execution outcomes. [10][20][21]

## Recent Advances (2024-2026)
Large-scale diffusion transformer models such as Sora and Veo 2 have pushed the frontier to minute-long, high-fidelity video generation with emergent capabilities like 3D consistency, long-range coherence, object permanence, and simulating digital worlds (e.g., Minecraft). [3][19] Sora is a text-conditional diffusion model trained jointly on videos and images of variable durations, resolutions, and aspect ratios, while Veo 2 demonstrates improved physics understanding, clearer clips, and better camera controls, hallucinating less than rivals. [3][19] Efficient open-source alternatives like Open-Sora democratize video production by introducing a Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer (STDiT) and a highly compressive 3D autoencoder, enabling video generation up to 15 seconds at 720p resolution with arbitrary aspect ratios using feasible training resources. [18] New benchmarks such as PEBench provide unified evaluation of prompt enhancers across text-to-video, image-to-video, and reference-to-video without costly video generation, speeding up iteration. [16] Procedural and streaming world models address long-horizon tasks: WorldGuide formulates procedural video generation as a closed-loop system deciding the next action from generated state, and SPW-Nav streams panoramic video for language-guided navigation in real time. [20][21] Audio-video separation and generation are advanced by models like SepGen, which produces accessible stem-level audio from joint audio-video generators. [26]

## Evaluation and Benchmarks
Evaluation has shifted from aggregate scores to multi-dimensional benchmark suites that decompose video generation quality into fine-grained aspects. VBench splits quality into 16 disentangled dimensions (e.g., subject identity consistency, motion smoothness, temporal flickering, spatial relationship) and includes human preference annotations to validate alignment with human perception. [11] Video-Bench divides quality into video quality (intrinsic fidelity) and video-condition alignment (adherence to input conditions), with the latter comprising object class consistency, action consistency, color consistency, scene consistency, and video-text consistency, and the former including imaging quality, aesthetic quality, temporal consistency, and motion quality, each evaluated with expert human raters. [12] VBench++ extends VBench with hierarchical dimensions, adaptive Image Suite for fair evaluation across text-to-video and image-to-video, and evaluation of trustworthiness. [13][14] AIGCBench offers another comprehensive evaluation framework with modules for dataset, metrics, and models, introducing 11 metrics across control-video alignment, motion effects, temporal consistency, and video quality. [15] Automatic metrics like VIDEOSCORE, trained on large-scale human feedback datasets, achieve high Spearman correlation with human judges (up to 77.1) and serve as proxies for fine-grained human feedback in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). [16] Prompt-enhancer benchmarks such as PEBench enable direct evaluation of prompt quality independent of downstream generator behavior, reducing computational and human costs. [17] For streaming perception in real-world contexts, FastBench evaluates high-dynamic perception in video streams for Streaming Video Large Language Models (VLMs) by combining QA generation from high-FPS clips, filtering, and verification under bounded context budgets. [17]

## Trends and Open Problems
Recent trends show scaling video generation models toward general-purpose world simulators, unification of multimodal generation under shared foundation models, and focus on efficient, long-video synthesis with better controllability and alignment. [3][19][20][21] Despite progress, open problems persist: achieving temporal coherence over long durations without error accumulation remains difficult; motion-appearance coupling complicates separate control of motion and appearance; computation efficiency hinders real-time applications; robust evaluation that fully aligns with human judgment is still elusive; safety, ethical concerns, and bias mitigation require attention; limited supervision for temporal aspects restricts learning of complex dynamics; and effective integration of audio and video generation (e.g., synchronized stem-level audio) is an emerging frontier. [10][7][8][9][1][26]
