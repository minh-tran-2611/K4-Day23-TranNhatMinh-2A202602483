# Survey of Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
- Small language models (SLMs) in the 100M–5B parameter range enable deployment on consumer devices with low latency and cost, retaining strong performance for specific tasks [1][2].
- Core efficiency techniques include quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, compact architectures (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts), and dynamic networks, each trading off accuracy, speed, and memory [3][4][5].
- Recent advances (2024–2026) introduce systems-level optimizations like DecDEC for low-bit quantization [6], EdgeVLA for vision-language-action inference [7], and collaborative frameworks such as SWARM-LLM for edge deployment [8].
- ProbeScale uses probing analysis to identify parameter-efficient subnetworks, optimizing the performance‑size trade‑off for SLM inference [9].
- Open challenges involve standardizing SLM definitions, bridging the performance gap with large models via test‑time compute, and ensuring trustworthiness in resource‑constrained settings [10][11][12].

## Background
Small language models (SLMs) are language models designed to fit on common consumer devices and serve agentic requests with low latency, a definition that evolves with hardware improvements [11]. They typically range from 100M to 5B parameters, offering strong performance on tasks like commonsense reasoning and mathematics while drastically reducing inference cost and energy use compared to large language models (LLMs) [2]. Foundational work such as DistilBERT demonstrated that knowledge distillation can shrink model size by 40% while retaining 97% of language understanding and enabling 60% faster inference [1]. SLMs can act as efficient proxies or reward models for LLMs, helping mitigate deployment, latency, and fine‑tuning challenges [10]. Emerging architectures like Mamba and xLSTM provide linear scaling versus the quadratic scaling of Transformers, further improving efficiency [10].

## Main Families of Efficient Inference Techniques
The primary techniques for efficient inference in SLMs and LLMs encompass quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, compact architecture design, and dynamic networks [3][4]. Quantization reduces memory cost and improves speed by mapping input values to a small set, and recent surveys examine its variants and applications [3]. Pruning removes unimportant components (neurons, layers) to cut memory and computational expenses [4]. Knowledge distillation transfers knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model, often using losses that combine task‑specific, distillation, and cosine‑distance objectives [1]. Compact architectures such as Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) activate only a subset of parameters per token, improving efficiency [10]. Dynamic networks adapt computation based on input, allocating more resources to difficult examples [4]. Layer‑Condensed KV Cache specifically targets the key‑value cache bottleneck in transformers, boosting inference throughput without performance loss [13][9]. Systematic reviews highlight the need to address scalability and integration gaps when combining compression with tuning strategies [14][15].

## Recent Advances (2024–2026)
Recent research has pushed efficient inference forward with both model‑level and systems‑level innovations. DecDEC improves low‑bit (3‑bit, 4‑bit) LLM quantization by storing residual matrices in CPU, preserving GPU memory savings and latency reduction while enhancing output quality [6]. EdgeVLA optimizes Vision‑Language‑Action (VLA) models for real‑time deployment on resource‑constrained mobile manipulation systems, maintaining representational power while accelerating inference [7]. ProbeScale proposes a probing analysis framework to identify parameter‑efficient subnetworks in SLMs, combining scaling law insights to optimize the performance‑size trade‑off for inference [9]. SWARM‑LLM enables collaborative inference for edge‑based SLMs by dynamically routing queries between local small models and a cloud foundation model, guided by uncertainty estimates and safety signals [8]. Surveys from 2024–2026 provide taxonomies and benchmarks for evaluating SLM efficiency, covering architectures, training techniques, and model compression methods [2][12].

## Trends and Open Problems
Over the last two years, the field has shifted toward holistic system‑software co‑design, exemplified by DecDEC’s CPU‑GPU hybrid approach and SWARM‑LLM’s dynamic cloud‑edge collaboration [6][8]. There is growing interest in vision‑language‑action models for robotics, driving specialized efficient inference techniques like EdgeVLA [7]. Despite progress, SLMs still lag behind LLMs in general capabilities; test‑time compute scaling strategies are being explored to narrow this gap [10]. Standardization remains an open issue, with calls for consistent definitions, acquisition practices, and reliability metrics for SLMs [10][11][12]. Trustworthiness—including fairness, robustness against adversarial attacks, and hardware‑specific optimizations—is increasingly important as compression techniques mature [15]. Finally, benchmarks that reflect real‑world edge deployment scenarios, measuring latency, energy, and FLOPs under realistic constraints, are needed to guide future work [2][12].
