# Survey of World Models: Foundations, Approaches, Recent Advances, and Applications

## TL;DR
- World models are internal simulators that learn environmental dynamics for prediction, planning, and reasoning, with roots in Craik (1943) and Schmidhuber (1990) [1][2].
- Architectural families include latent recurrent models (e.g., DreamerV3), JEPA-based models, transformer-based models, and diffusion-based generators, each differing in representation and training [3][4][5][6].
- Recent advances (2024-2026) focus on action‑faithful robot world models, scaling ego‑centric video data, goal‑directed procedural video models, and sparse imagination for efficient planning [7][8][9][10].
- World models enable robot learning, policy planning, and simulation across embodied applications, supported by surveys and benchmarks [11][12][13].
- Open challenges include improving object‑level fidelity, long‑horizon task execution, and integrating multimodal sensory inputs while maintaining computational efficiency [14][15][16].

## Background
The concept of a world model as an internal simulator dates back to Craik’s 1943 proposal that biological organisms hold “working models” to simulate hypothetical scenarios and predict outcomes [1]. In machine learning, Jürgen Schmidhuber introduced the term in 1990, advocating recurrent neural networks that predict future states from observations [2]. Yann LeCun (2022) later argued that intelligence requires predictive world models and proposed the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) as a practical foundation [1][2]. World models serve two primary functions: constructing internal representations to understand the present state of the world, and predicting future states to simulate and guide decision‑making [12]. They have become essential for robot learning, supporting policy learning, planning, and simulation across embodied applications [11]. The rise of foundation models and large‑scale video generation has accelerated advances in the field [11].

## Architectural Families and Approaches
World models are categorized along multiple axes, including architecture, methodological family, reasoning strategy, and application domain [3]. Architectural dimensions encompass representation format, dynamics formulation, input modality, learning paradigm, and downstream use cases [3]. Methodological families include state‑space and recurrent latent models (e.g., PlaNet, Dreamer family), variational and generative approaches, transformer‑based models, diffusion‑based generators, physics‑informed networks, and language‑augmented multimodal systems [3]. Historical milestone systems traced in surveys include PlaNet, the Dreamer family, MuZero, Sora, Cosmos, and Genie [3].

Latent recurrent models such as DreamerV3 learn compact recurrent policies with a 64‑dimensional state, combining diagonal linear recurrence and optional rank‑$k$ nonlinear innovation while retaining nonlinear observation encoders and action heads [13]. World‑action models (WAMs) predict actions and future observations; LeWAM introduces a bidirectional transformer for forward, backward, inverse dynamics and policy prediction on a decoder‑free JEPA latent trained end‑to‑end through all four modes, improving alignment of linear probes to read robot and object state from the latent [4]. For long video‑language sequences, RingAttention enables scalable training on million‑length contexts, improving retrieval and long‑form understanding tasks [5]. GE‑Act 2.0 (Genie Envisioner Act 2.0) combines a control‑oriented autoencoder, a single‑step visual planner, and an inverse dynamics model, retaining action‑ and instruction‑relevant features [6].

## Recent Advances (2024‑2026)
Recent work emphasizes action‑faithful and physically plausible video prediction for robotics. DreamTrue is a multi‑view, cross‑embodiment robot world model that introduces offline geometric calibration and renders action trajectories into image‑space conditions to improve action following across embodiments [7]. Scaling ego‑centric human video to 30,000 hours improves agent modeling fidelity, though object‑level interactions remain low and improve slowly [14]. OuroWorld turns static 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes into 3D cinemagraphs with vivid, diverse motion looping seamlessly from any viewpoint, guided by a vision‑language model [8].

Goal‑directed procedural task execution is addressed by WorldGuide, which formulates procedural video generation as a closed‑loop system where the model determines the next action from its generated state, executes it, and recognizes task completion, adapting to execution outcomes [9]. Chain of World (CoWVLA) combines world‑model temporal reasoning with disentangled latent motion representation to improve visuomotor learning efficiency in robotic systems [15]. Sparse Imagination reduces the number of tokens processed during forward prediction in visual world model planning by leveraging a sparsely trained vision‑based world model with randomized grouped attention, aiming to alleviate computational burden in resource‑constrained robotics [10]. Critiques of existing world models propose hierarchical, multi‑level, mixed continuous/discrete representations to simulate actionable possibilities for purposeful reasoning and acting [16].

## Applications and Evaluations
World models support policy learning, planning, simulation, evaluation, and data generation for robot learning [11]. In robot air hockey, a DreamerV3 teacher outperforms a memoryless policy under temporary loss of puck tracking, demonstrating the necessity of memory; resetting the teacher's recurrent state sharply reduces performance, indicating the importance of recurrent dynamics [13]. GE‑Act 2.0 enables learning from both action‑free video and action‑labeled interaction data for robotic manipulation [6]. WorldGuide’s closed‑loop formulation improves long‑horizon procedural task execution by overcoming limitations of open‑loop generation and reliance on pretrained executors [9]. Surveys provide benchmarks and resource lists for world models in robotics [11][12]. Evaluation often relies on challenging out‑of‑distribution benchmarks for agent and object‑interaction fidelity [14].

## Trends and Open Problems
Trends over the last two years include scaling ego‑centric video data, developing action‑faithful models for cross‑embodiment robotics, and integrating vision‑language guidance for procedural tasks [14][7][8][9]. There is growing interest in efficient planning via sparse imagination and latent motion representations to reduce computational load [15][10]. However, object‑level fidelity remains low even with massive data scaling, suggesting that scale alone is insufficient for complex interaction modeling [14]. Long‑horizon task execution and generalization to novel environments remain challenging, prompting closed‑loop and goal‑directed approaches [9]. Integrating multimodal sensory inputs (e.g., tactile, auditory) while preserving real‑time performance is an open direction. Additionally, benchmarks that accurately measure physical plausibility and long‑term consistency are needed to guide progress.
